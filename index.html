📖 CARS Model Fundamentals

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand the three moves of the CARS model and their purposes
  • Identify linguistic signals and strategies for each move
  • Apply CARS model principles to your own research writing
  • Recognize and avoid common errors in research introductions
  • Analyze and improve existing research article introductions

Move 1: Establishing Territory

Claiming centrality and making topic generalizations about the research area.

Click to reveal strategies →

📌 Territory Strategies

  • Claiming centrality: Show topic importance (e.g., "Recent years have witnessed growing interest in...")
  • Making topic generalizations: State established knowledge (e.g., "It is generally accepted that...")
  • Reviewing previous research: Cite key studies (e.g., "Smith (2020) and Jones (2021) have shown...")

Move 2: Establishing a Niche

Indicating a gap, raising questions, or challenging existing knowledge.

Click to reveal strategies →

🎯 Niche Strategies

  • Counter-claiming: Challenge existing views (e.g., "However, recent evidence suggests...")
  • Indicating a gap: Show what's missing (e.g., "Despite extensive research, little attention has been paid to...")
  • Question-raising: Pose unanswered questions (e.g., "A key question that remains is...")

Move 3: Occupying the Niche

Outlining purposes, announcing research, and indicating structure.

Click to reveal strategies →

💡 Occupation Strategies

  • Outlining purposes: State study aims (e.g., "The purpose of this study is to...")
  • Announcing present research: Introduce your study (e.g., "This paper reports on...")
  • Indicating structure: Preview paper organization (e.g., "The paper is organized as follows...")

📝 Interactive Example

❌ Weak Introduction (Without CARS):

"This paper is about online learning. Online learning is important. I studied online learning in universities."
Problems: No territory establishment, vague statements, no niche identification, weak occupation.

✅ Strong Introduction (Using CARS):

[Territory] Recent years have witnessed growing scholarly interest in online learning environments, with numerous studies documenting their potential to enhance educational access and flexibility (Smith, 2020; Johnson, 2021).

[Niche] However, despite this increased attention, relatively little research has examined the long-term retention effects of asynchronous micro-learning modules, particularly in higher education contexts.

[Occupation] This study therefore aims to investigate the relationship between micro-learning frequency and six-month knowledge retention among university students, using a mixed-methods approach combining quantitative assessment with qualitative interviews.

Strengths: Clear territory establishment, specific niche identification, detailed occupation with methodology.

🃏 CARS Model Flashcards

What is the primary purpose of Move 1 (Establishing Territory) in the CARS model?

Click the card or use the Flip button to see the answer

Answer:

The primary purpose of Move 1 is to establish the importance and centrality of the research topic, showing readers why it matters and situating it within existing scholarship. This involves claiming centrality, making topic generalizations, and reviewing previous research to create a context for the study.

Corrected Version:

"Recent years have witnessed growing scholarly interest in online learning environments, with numerous studies documenting their potential to enhance educational access and flexibility (Smith, 2020). However, despite this increased attention, relatively little research has examined the long-term retention effects of asynchronous micro-learning modules in higher education. This study therefore aims to investigate the relationship between micro-learning frequency and six-month knowledge retention among university students, using a mixed-methods approach. The paper is organized as follows: first, we review relevant literature; second, we describe our methodology; third, we present findings; and finally, we discuss implications."
